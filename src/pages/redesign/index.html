The Barthel Agency — Website Refresh Concept

A working prototype of what the agency’s online presence could become, built from the agency’s real public information, photos, and history. Prepared as a sales concept — every page in this site is live and clickable.

The opportunity

The Barthel Agency has been part of Princeton since 1973. It has real specialists, a real office on Main Street, real carrier relationships, and a client base that spans generations. Its current website, however, is a managed template that tells none of that story. For a business whose reputation is personal, the website undersells everything that makes it worth choosing.

This concept shows what a focused rebuild looks like when it starts from the agency’s actual strengths: longevity, people, local context, and range.

What we noticed about the current site

Said constructively — most of this is common to managed template platforms, and none of it is hard to fix:

  • Inconsistent HTTPS. The site serves over plain HTTP, and modern browsers that try to upgrade the connection get a certificate error instead of the website — some visitors likely never see the site at all.
  • Template defaults. Stock hero photography of models in a coffee shop stands in for the actual office, town, and team.
  • Generic copy dominates. The homepage text is boilerplate about “a wealth of information in the form of newsletter articles” — the agency’s identity, history, and people are invisible.
  • Team page spacing. Uneven photo crops, inconsistent card heights, and empty gaps make a genuinely strong asset (a real, local staff) look unfinished.
  • Weak mobile experience. The small-screen view leads with a generic stock-market slideshow and boilerplate widgets; on arrival there is no phone number, no address, and no agency identity in sight.
  • Unclear conversion path. Quote links exist (seven of them) but live in a dropdown, not anywhere a first-time visitor would look.

What already works

  • Genuine history. Established 1973 — verifiable, and rare among local agencies.
  • A real team. Chris (CFP®, ChFC®), Susan, and Jennifer (CISR Elite) are the kind of people clients actually ask for by name.
  • Breadth of service. Auto, home, business, life, health/Medicare, employee benefits, and financial services under one roof.
  • Working integrations. Existing online quote forms (auto, home, business, health) function today — the new design routes to them cleanly.
  • An established local footprint. Facebook, LinkedIn, carrier directories, and the courthouse-square location all reinforce legitimacy.

The new direction

  • Own the identity. Navy and gold drawn directly from the agency’s existing logo; serif headlines that read “established,” not “startup.”
  • Lead with the answer. What they do, where they are, why it matters — visible before the first scroll.
  • Real photography. The actual Main Street office and the actual staff — no stock models.
  • Make contact frictionless. Phone number pinned in the top bar and hero; quote links surfaced on every service page.
  • Fast, accessible, modern. Hand-built static pages, no framework bloat, semantic HTML, keyboard-friendly navigation, WCAG-AA-minded contrast.

Proposed scope of a production build

This prototype already covers most of it. The remainder is deployment work.

Included in this concept

  • Full responsive site — 13 content pages + 404
  • Team, about, claims & service, resources
  • Quote center routing to existing carrier forms
  • Local-business + person structured data
  • Sitemap, robots, canonical URLs, Open Graph
  • Accessibility pass (keyboard, contrast, semantics)

Production deployment work

  • HTTPS-only hosting with certificate for required hostnames
  • HTTP→HTTPS 301 redirects + canonical host + HSTS
  • 301 map for legacy URLs (team.htm, contact_us/, etc.)
  • Real form backend for contact & quote requests
  • Analytics (GA4/privacy-friendly) + Search Console
  • Content handoff & compliance review with the agency

Honest by design: the contact form on this prototype validates input but doesn’t transmit anywhere — nothing is faked. Quote links route to the agency’s existing live provider forms.
